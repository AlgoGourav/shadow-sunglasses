# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Gen Z glasses-wearers who want their everyday specs to also work as sunglasses — affordably and stylishly. They're on the fence between "buy actual prescription sunglasses" (expensive, one color forever) and doing nothing. The job to be done: turn the glasses they already own into sunglasses, and be able to change the tint/color on a whim without buying a new pair.

## Product Purpose

Shadow-web sells "Shades" — peelable lens films that stick to the lens of any glasses. The primary value is functional: real sunglasses performance (light/UV blocking), not decoration. The secondary value, and the hook, is that tints/colors can be swapped inexpensively for an aesthetic upgrade whenever the user wants a new look.

A first-time visitor should immediately understand: "this turns any glasses into sunglasses, and you can swap colors cheaply." They should never read the product as a decorative sticker or novelty add-on — that framing undersells it and reads cheap.

Success = visitor grasps the functional value in seconds, then gets pulled in by the color-swap hook, then shops.

### Confirmed application mechanics

Owner-confirmed October 1, 2026: each film is pre-cut to the corresponding lens, adheres to the **outside/front** surface, and covers the full usable lens area to the rim. The original clear prescription lens stays installed. A removed film is not reusable; changing the tint means applying a fresh film.

## Brand Personality

Modern, Gen Z, cool. Distinct, bold typography is a core identity trait — type should carry as much brand weight as color or imagery. Confident and premium in tone, never juvenile or gimmicky.

## Anti-references

None specified by name. Avoid any visual or copy language that reads as "sticker pack" / craft-decal / novelty-decoration — cheapens a functional product. Avoid generic SaaS-cream, hero-metric, or templated e-commerce clichés.

## Design Principles

- **Premium, not cheap**: language and visuals never lean on "sticker" connotations (no scrapbook textures, no peel-corner clip art clichés). Say "shades" / "lens tints", not "stickers".
- **Function-first clarity**: the sunglasses transformation is the headline message; color-swapping is the hook that follows, not the lead.
- **Typography as hero device**: bold, distinct type carries brand identity — not just a background palette or icon set.
- **Product-is-the-visual**: let real product/lens/glasses imagery do the persuading rather than illustrative decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
